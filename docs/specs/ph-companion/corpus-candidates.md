## How this list was made

- Source: Europe PMC search (which includes PubMed), 23 queries across topics and cohorts, sorted by citation count.
- Every paper below is a real record; the number in the first column is its PubMed ID (`https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/<id>/`).
- Design, sample size and cohort were read **once, from the abstract only**. They have not had the second check yet. Where the abstract did not state a value it is marked "—" and must be filled from the paper.
- "Open" means Europe PMC lists the paper as open access with the licence shown. Others can be cited and linked, but only their abstract may be stored.

## Result in short

| Persona | Matched candidates | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 29, Indian, premenopausal | 9 studies in Indian women of reproductive age, plus 3 in the broader South Asian group | Well covered for bacterial vaginosis and vaginal bacteria. **Thin for the actual question**: no candidate clearly reports a normal pH range for healthy Indian women |
| 56, postmenopausal, no HRT | 11 studies and reviews on pH and the vaginal environment after menopause | Well covered, including pH values with and without oestrogen |
| 33, Black, pregnant | 15 studies in pregnant women and/or Black women, several very large | The best-covered persona. Includes pH by race during pregnancy |

Three findings worth knowing before the personas are final:

1. **The expected gap is not where we assumed.** Indian studies exist in good number. What is missing is narrower: pH reference values in healthy Indian women, and almost anything on women of Indian ancestry living outside South Asia (one Dutch study with a South-Asian Surinamese group). If the Indian persona lives in Europe, the honest answer is "studies in India exist; studies in women like you living here barely do". That is a strong demo moment.
2. **The small-study example is real.** Several matched studies have 38–80 participants (Indian cohorts) and one has 40 (Black pregnant women), next to general studies with thousands.
3. **A fourth combination is unexpectedly well covered:** postmenopausal Indian women (two studies, 173 and 120 women, both on pH). Worth considering if you want a persona where two attributes match at once.

Caution on one famous paper: Ravel 2011 reports pH for an "Asian" group in North America. The abstract does not say this group is South Asian, so it must not be counted as a match for the Indian persona until the paper is checked.

## Gaps found

| Gap | Effect on the demo |
|---|---|
| No candidate clearly gives a normal pH range for healthy premenopausal Indian women | The Indian persona's reference range comes from general studies; matched studies cover bacteria and bacterial vaginosis. To be confirmed by reading the five most promising papers in group A |
| Women of South Asian ancestry living in Europe or North America: one study | If the persona lives in Europe, the answer should say so |
| Postmenopausal Black women: nothing specific found | Not needed for the current personas |
| Pregnancy studies in South Asian women report bacteria, not pH | Not needed for the current personas |
| pH after intercourse and across the cycle: old or very small studies (one with 3 women) | The answer should word these cautiously |

## Guidelines (task 0.4)

Identified so far: the CDC 2021 treatment guideline (through its evidence summary), the European IUSTI/WHO 2018 guideline on vaginal discharge, and the 2025 AUA/SUFU/AUGS guideline on menopause-related symptoms. Still to look for: the US screening recommendation on bacterial vaginosis in pregnancy, and a German guideline. Licences are not reviewed for the demo.
